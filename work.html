Problem

A fast-growing fintech in financial services had a delinquent portfolio that kept expanding — not because recovery was impossible, but because internal records and stakeholder records didn't match. Every conversation with a counterparty stalled on "whose number is right?" Recovery was artisanal, slow, and bottlenecked on individual analyst memory.

Approach

Build a reconciliation engine first — a single, automated source of truth for every disputed record — and then use its output as leverage in recovery negotiations. In parallel, re-shape how the team operated: new KPIs, tighter SLAs, real documentation, and mentorship for the entry-level analysts who'd own the process.

Solution (technical & organizational)
  • ▸ SQL + Python matching engine reconciling internal vs. stakeholder records automatically
  • ▸ Reconciliation dashboards in Power BI for recovery operators
  • ▸ New KPI framework + follow-up methodology to shorten SLAs
  • ▸ Documentation practices introduced across the conciliation team
  • ▸ Led negotiations with delinquent counterparties using engine output
  • ▸ Represented the team in BI leadership meetings to influence strategy
Impact

Recovered 50M+ MXN of delinquent portfolio through data-led negotiations. Follow-up SLAs shortened via the new methodology. Reconciliation became a systematic operation instead of depending on individual heroics, and the analyst team was left with clearer KPIs, better documentation, and a playbook they could own.

reconcile.py
# automated record matching
def reconcile(internal, stakeholder):
  matched = join(internal, stakeholder)
  gaps = find_gaps(matched)
  return gaps.rank(by="recovery_value")

$ python reconcile.py
# > recovered: 50,000,000+ MXN

Problem

The client — a fintech operating in financial services — was planning critical cash flows by hand. Forecasts were late, noisy, and siloed in spreadsheets. In parallel, fraud was being spotted after the fact: suspicious transactions surfaced only in next-day reports, and the risk team had no early-warning signal to act on.

Approach

Two tracks ran in parallel. The first: an end-to-end ML pipeline that took the forecasting work out of spreadsheets and into production. The second: an automated fraud-detection layer that turned scattered transaction data into real-time alerts the risk team could act on. Both built to be owned, not rented.

Solution (technical)
  • ▸ End-to-end forecasting pipeline: ingestion → features → model → delivery
  • ▸ Supervised ML models tuned for critical financial-flow horizons
  • ▸ Automated fraud detection: SQL/Python rules flagging suspicious user transactions in real time
  • ▸ Email-based alerting wired directly to the risk team's workflow
  • ▸ Full income analytics lifecycle for a fintech subsidiary
  • ▸ Multiple ETL processes for operational and financial teams, with the relevant KPIs baked in
  • ▸ Compliance-sensitive automations reducing processing time and human error
Impact

Operational planning moved from manual guesswork to forecasts running consistently under 15% MAPE. Fraud exposure dropped as the risk team moved from next-day reporting to real-time notifications. Compliance-sensitive processes ran faster, with less human error and lower regulatory risk — and the income analytics program shipped insights that shaped strategy at the fintech subsidiary.

Problem

Approach

Solution (technical)
  • ▸ Target data architecture: sources → warehouse → analytics-ready models → BI
  • ▸ ETL/ELT pipelines on Azure + Databricks, orchestrated and monitored
  • ▸ Analytics-ready data models for leasing operations, finance, and risk

Arrendamex now has a working data architecture, first ETL/ELT pipelines in production, and a BI foundation operational teams actually use. Reporting that used to live in spreadsheets is automated; data-driven decisions are replacing gut-feel in operations and finance. Engagement is ongoing.

Problem

Inveravocados Jalmich ran a growing avocado export & distribution business on gut-feel planning. Stockouts at packing and downstream distribution lost them ~8% of potential revenue quarterly. Previous forecasting attempts lived in an analyst's notebook and never got adopted by operations.

Approach

I spent a week on-site shadowing packing and logistics leads. The forecasting model wasn't the hard part — the hard part was designing an interface operators would open on a Monday morning and actually trust.

Solution (technical)
  • ▸ Daily harvest + inventory + distribution pipeline into Snowflake via Azure Data Factory
  • ▸ Hierarchical forecasting: LightGBM with seasonality + harvest-cycle features
  • ▸ Weekly retraining pipeline + automated accuracy scorecards
  • ▸ Streamlit app: SKU × destination forecast, override + justify workflow
  • ▸ WhatsApp / Slack alerts for expected stockouts 7 days out
Impact

35% fewer stockouts. 19% margin improvement from better inventory turns. 92% of operations leads open the app on Monday mornings. Inveravocados Jalmich now uses the platform as a selling point with buyers and distribution partners.

forecast_eval.py
# weekly accuracy scorecard
for site in sites:
  mape = eval(site, horizon=7)
  if mape > 0.22:
    alerts.notify(site.lead)

$ python forecast_eval.py
# avg MAPE = 0.18 · 4,200 SKUs
